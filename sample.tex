		\paragraph\ \ \ \ Artificial intelligence based speech synthesis systems have advanced significantly in recent years, enabling the generation of highly realistic synthetic voices. While these technologies provide many beneficial applications such as virtual assistants and automated communication systems, they also introduce serious security risks. AI generated voices can be used in impersonation attacks, fraudulent phone calls and social engineering scams where attackers imitate trusted individuals. Detecting such synthetic speech during live communication has therefore become an important cybersecurity challenge.

Existing AI voice detection solutions often rely on large cloud based machine learning models that require significant computational resources and internet connectivity. These approaches introduce latency and raise privacy concerns since user audio must be transmitted to external servers for analysis. Furthermore, many existing detection systems are designed for offline analysis and are not optimized for real time detection during active calls.

This project proposes a lightweight real time system capable of detecting AI generated voices directly on the user device. The system continuously monitors active voice calls, captures audio streams and processes them in small frames for analysis. Acoustic features including Mel Spectrograms and Linear Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (LFCC) are extracted from the speech signal. A Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) learns discriminative patterns from Mel Spectrogram features while a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) statistically models LFCC features to capture characteristics of synthetic speech.

To improve reliability and reduce prediction noise, a hybrid decision engine aggregates predictions across multiple frames using a sliding window mechanism. By combining CNN and GMM outputs, the system achieves more robust detection compared to single model approaches. The entire detection pipeline operates locally on the device, ensuring low latency, improved privacy and efficient performance.

The final detection results are presented through a graphical interface that provides real time classification and warning notifications when AI generated voices are detected, thereby helping protect users from emerging deepfake voice scams.

	\chapter{Introduction}
	
	\section{Background}
	
	The rapid advancement of artificial intelligence has led to the development of highly sophisticated speech synthesis systems capable of generating human-like voice output that is nearly indistinguishable from natural speech. Technologies such as text-to-speech (TTS) engines, voice cloning frameworks, and neural vocoders have made it possible to replicate a person's voice with only a few seconds of reference audio. While these technologies have beneficial applications in accessibility, entertainment, and automated communication, they also introduce significant cybersecurity risks. Attackers can exploit AI-generated voices to impersonate trusted individuals during phone calls, enabling social engineering attacks, financial fraud, and identity theft.
	
	Voice over Internet Protocol (VoIP) platforms such as WhatsApp, Zoom, Microsoft Teams, and Discord have become the dominant medium for personal and professional communication. The prevalence of these platforms, combined with the accessibility of AI voice generation tools, has created a new category of threat known as deepfake voice scams. According to industry reports, losses from voice-based fraud have increased substantially in recent years, with AI-generated voices being used to deceive victims into transferring funds or revealing sensitive information. The detection of such synthetic speech in real time during active voice calls has therefore become a pressing cybersecurity challenge that demands practical, deployable solutions.
	
	
	\section{Problem Definition}
	
	Current AI voice detection methods predominantly rely on large cloud-based models that introduce network latency, require internet connectivity, and raise privacy concerns by transmitting user audio to external servers. There is a need for a lightweight, on-device system that can detect AI-generated voices in real time during active VoIP calls without relying on cloud infrastructure, thereby ensuring low latency, user privacy, and efficient resource utilization.
	
	
	\section{Scope and Motivation}
	
	The scope of this project encompasses the design, development, and evaluation of a complete end-to-end system for real-time detection of AI-generated voices during live VoIP calls. The system operates entirely on the user's local device, capturing audio from active calls using Windows Audio Session API (WASAPI) loopback, extracting acoustic features, performing inference using dual machine learning models, and presenting results through a graphical user interface. The detection pipeline covers the entire workflow from VoIP call detection and audio capture through feature extraction, model inference, ensemble decision-making, and user notification.
	
	The motivation for this work arises from the growing prevalence of deepfake voice scams that exploit the trust inherent in voice communication. Existing detection solutions are either too computationally expensive for real-time use, require cloud connectivity that compromises user privacy, or are designed only for offline forensic analysis. The availability of consumer-grade hardware capable of running lightweight neural networks in real time, combined with the TensorFlow Lite framework for model optimization, creates an opportunity to build a practical, privacy-preserving detection tool. This project aims to bridge the gap between academic deepfake detection research and real-world deployable solutions that can protect everyday users.

	\section{Objectives}
	
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item To design and implement a real-time audio capture system that monitors active VoIP calls using WASAPI loopback recording on Windows.
		\item To develop a feature extraction pipeline that computes Mel Spectrograms for CNN-based classification and Linear Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (LFCC) for GMM-based classification.
		\item To train a lightweight Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) using depthwise separable convolutions that achieves high detection accuracy with minimal computational overhead.
		\item To train a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) on LFCC features to provide a complementary statistical classification approach.
		\item To implement a hybrid decision engine that aggregates CNN and GMM predictions across multiple audio frames using a sliding window mechanism for robust classification.
		\item To deploy the trained hybrid model in TensorFlow Lite format for optimized on-device inference and present detection results through a real-time graphical user interface with visual alerts.
        \item To enable community-driven reporting of detected AI scam calls to build a shared database of suspicious callers.
	\end{enumerate}
	
	\section{Challenges}
	
	The primary challenge is achieving reliable detection accuracy under the severe audio degradation introduced by VoIP codecs, particularly the Opus codec used by WhatsApp and other popular platforms. Codec compression at low bitrates (16--48 kbps) removes high-frequency details that are often the distinguishing markers of AI-generated speech. Additionally, maintaining real-time performance (under 100ms per inference window) on consumer hardware while running dual models in parallel requires careful optimization of the feature extraction and inference pipelines.
	
	
	\section{Assumptions}
	
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item The system operates on a Windows 10/11 device with access to the WASAPI loopback audio interface for capturing system audio output.
		\item VoIP applications produce audio sessions that can be detected through the Windows audio session API or microphone activity monitoring.
		\item The incoming speech signal contains sufficient non-silent segments (at least 60\% non-silence) for meaningful feature extraction and classification.
	\end{enumerate}
	
	
	\section{Societal / Industrial Relevance}
	
	This project directly addresses the growing threat of AI-powered voice fraud, which impacts individuals, financial institutions, and organizations worldwide. For society, the system provides a free, privacy-preserving tool that can protect vulnerable populations --- particularly elderly individuals who are disproportionately targeted by phone scams --- from deepfake voice attacks. The on-device nature of the solution ensures that personal conversations are never transmitted to third-party servers, respecting user privacy while providing security.
	
	From an industrial perspective, the lightweight architecture and TFLite deployment model make the system suitable for integration into VoIP applications, call centers, and enterprise communication platforms. Financial institutions and customer service providers can deploy similar technology to verify the authenticity of voice communications, reducing fraud losses. The community-based flagging system, which uses hashed phone numbers and a cloud database for crowd-sourced threat intelligence, demonstrates a scalable approach to collective defence against voice-based attacks.

	\section{Introduction}
	\paragraph\ The Software Requirements Specification (SRS) document describes the functional and non-functional requirements of the Real-Time Lightweight On-Device Detection of AI-Generated Voice Calls system. The system is a desktop application targeting Windows 10/11 that monitors active VoIP calls, captures audio in real time, and classifies incoming speech as human or AI-generated using an ensemble of CNN and GMM models. The intended users are individuals who wish to protect themselves from deepfake voice scams during everyday communication.
	
	\subsection{Purpose}
	The purpose of this document is to provide a detailed description of the requirements for the AI voice detection system. It specifies the software interfaces, functional capabilities, performance constraints, and design requirements that guide the development process.
	
	\subsection{Product Scope}
	The system, named \textbf{Das-Detects}, is a standalone Windows desktop application that:
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Automatically detects active VoIP calls (WhatsApp, Zoom, Teams, Discord, etc.)
		\item Captures incoming audio via WASAPI loopback recording
		\item Extracts Mel Spectrogram and LFCC features from audio frames
		\item Classifies speech using a TFLite CNN model and a GMM model
		\item Aggregates predictions using a sliding window decision engine
		\item Displays real-time classification results with visual alerts
		\item Optionally queries a community cloud database for flagged caller numbers
	\end{itemize}
	
	\subsection{Definitions and Acronyms}
	Refer to the List of Abbreviations provided at the beginning of this report.
	
	
	\section{Overall Description}
	
	\subsection{Product Perspective}
	\paragraph\ Das-Detects is an independent desktop application that operates alongside existing VoIP software. It does not modify or intercept network traffic; instead, it passively captures the audio output of the operating system using the WASAPI loopback interface. The system architecture is modular, comprising separate components for call detection, audio recording, feature extraction, model inference, decision aggregation, and user interface rendering.
	
	\subsection{Product Functions}
	The major functions of the system are:
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item \textbf{VoIP Call Detection}: Continuously monitors Windows audio sessions and microphone activity to detect when a VoIP call is active.
		\item \textbf{Audio Capture}: Records system audio output at 48 kHz using loopback recording, converting to 16 kHz mono for processing.
		\item \textbf{Feature Extraction}: Computes 60-band Mel Spectrograms (for CNN) and 20-coefficient LFCC features (for GMM) from 2.5-second audio windows.
		\item \textbf{CNN Inference}: Runs a depthwise separable CNN via TensorFlow Lite to produce an AI probability score.
		\item \textbf{GMM Inference}: Scores audio against trained human and AI Gaussian Mixture Models to produce a complementary probability score.
		\item \textbf{Ensemble Decision}: Combines CNN (60\% weight) and GMM (40\% weight) scores with a 5-frame sliding window for smoothed classification.
		\item \textbf{User Interface}: Displays real-time status, confidence scores, waveform visualization, and warning notifications.
		\item \textbf{Community Flagging}: Optionally checks caller numbers (hashed with SHA-256) against a Supabase cloud database of reported scam numbers.
	\end{enumerate}
	
	\subsection{User Characteristics}
	The target users are general computer users with no specialized technical knowledge. The system is designed to be fully automatic --- requiring no manual configuration. Users simply launch the application, and it begins monitoring for calls.
	
	\subsection{Constraints}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item The system requires Windows 10 or later with WASAPI support.
		\item Audio capture depends on the VoIP application producing audio through the default audio output device.
		\item The system cannot capture audio from end-to-end encrypted calls at the network layer; it relies on system audio output.
		\item Internet connectivity is required only for the optional community flagging feature.
	\end{itemize}
	
	\subsection{Assumptions and Dependencies}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Python 3.10 or later is installed on the system.
		\item Required libraries (TensorFlow, librosa, soundcard, pycaw, customtkinter) are installed.
		\item The operating system permits loopback audio capture and microphone access monitoring.
	\end{itemize}
	
	\section{External Interface Requirements}
	
	\subsection{User Interfaces}
	\paragraph\ The system provides a graphical user interface built with the \texttt{customtkinter} framework featuring a dark-mode design. The main window displays: (a) a call status banner indicating whether a VoIP call is detected, (b) an animated waveform visualization showing audio activity, (c) a large status label showing the current classification (HUMAN, SUSPICIOUS, or AI GENERATED), (d) a confidence percentage with color-coded display, (e) a 5-element buffer indicator showing per-frame classification history, and (f) threat warning banners when community-flagged numbers are detected.
	
	\subsection{Hardware Interfaces}
	\paragraph\ The system interfaces with the default audio output device through the WASAPI loopback API provided by the Windows operating system. No specialized hardware is required. The system is designed to run on standard consumer laptops and desktops with a minimum of 4 GB RAM and a multi-core CPU.
	
	\subsection{Software Interfaces}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item \textbf{Windows WASAPI}: For audio session enumeration and loopback recording.
		\item \textbf{TensorFlow Lite Runtime}: For CNN model inference.
		\item \textbf{scikit-learn}: For GMM model loading and scoring.
		\item \textbf{Supabase REST API}: For community blocklist queries (optional).
		\item \textbf{librosa}: For audio resampling and feature computation.
	\end{itemize}
	
	\subsection{Communication Interfaces}
	\paragraph\ The only network communication occurs when the optional community flagging feature is enabled, which makes HTTPS REST API calls to a Supabase PostgreSQL backend. Phone numbers are hashed with SHA-256 before transmission to preserve privacy.
	
	\section{System Features}
	
	\subsection{FR-01: Automatic VoIP Call Detection}
	\paragraph\ The system shall automatically detect when a VoIP call is active by monitoring Windows audio sessions for known VoIP process keywords (WhatsApp, Zoom, Teams, Discord, Telegram, Signal, Skype, Viber, Slack, WebEx) and checking microphone activity status. The system shall use hysteresis logic to prevent false triggering from voice message playback.
	
	\subsection{FR-02: Real-Time Audio Capture}
	\paragraph\ Upon detecting an active call, the system shall begin recording audio from the default speaker device using WASAPI loopback at the system sample rate (48 kHz). Audio shall be captured in 3-second frames, converted to mono, and placed in a thread-safe queue with a maximum capacity of 20 frames.
	
	\subsection{FR-03: Audio Preprocessing}
	\paragraph\ Each captured frame shall undergo preprocessing including silence detection (frames with $>$40\% silence are discarded), resampling from 48 kHz to 16 kHz, trimming or padding to a fixed 2.5-second window (40,000 samples), and RMS normalization to a target level of $-$20 dB.
	
	\subsection{FR-04: Dual-Model Inference}
	\paragraph\ The system shall perform inference using two parallel classification models: (a) a CNN operating on Mel Spectrogram features via TensorFlow Lite, and (b) a GMM operating on LFCC features. Each model shall independently produce a probability score indicating the likelihood that the audio is AI-generated.
	
	\subsection{FR-05: Ensemble Decision Engine}
	\paragraph\ The system shall combine CNN and GMM probability scores using weighted averaging (CNN: 60\%, GMM: 40\%) and maintain a 5-frame sliding window buffer. The smoothed average score shall be classified as: Human ($<$0.40), Suspicious (0.40--0.70), or AI Generated ($\geq$0.70). The system shall generate a one-shot notification upon the first detection of AI-generated speech after the buffer is filled.
	
	\subsection{FR-06: User Interface Display}
	\paragraph\ The system shall display real-time classification results through a GUI that includes animated waveform visualization, color-coded confidence display (green for human, yellow for suspicious, red for AI), a per-frame buffer indicator, and warning banners for detected threats.
	
	\section{Other Nonfunctional Requirements}
	
	\subsection{Performance Requirements}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item The CNN inference shall complete in under 50 ms per 2.5-second audio window.
		\item The GMM inference shall complete in under 25 ms per window.
		\item The total end-to-end latency from audio capture to UI update shall not exceed 100 ms.
		\item The TFLite model file shall not exceed 400 KB in size.
	\end{itemize}
	
	\subsection{Safety and Security Requirements}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item All audio processing shall occur locally on the device; no audio data shall be transmitted over the network.
		\item Caller phone numbers shall be hashed using SHA-256 before any cloud database interaction.
		\item The system shall not store or log raw audio recordings.
	\end{itemize}
	
	\subsection{Software Quality Attributes}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item \textbf{Reliability}: The system shall maintain classification accuracy above 95\% on VoIP-degraded audio.
		\item \textbf{Usability}: The system shall operate without requiring any user configuration or manual intervention.
		\item \textbf{Portability}: The core detection pipeline shall be designed for future adaptation to mobile platforms via TFLite.
		\item \textbf{Maintainability}: The codebase shall follow a modular architecture with clearly separated components for each stage of the pipeline.
	\end{itemize}
	

	
	
	
	\chapter{System Architecture and Design}
	\section{System Overview }
	\paragraph\ 
	The Das-Detects system is an end-to-end pipeline for real-time detection of AI-generated voices during active VoIP calls. The system operates as a background monitoring service that automatically activates when a VoIP call is detected, processes audio in fixed-length windows, and presents classification results through a real-time graphical interface.
	
	The overall data flow proceeds as follows: (1) the VoIP Monitor module continuously polls Windows audio sessions to detect active calls; (2) upon detection, the Audio Recorder module captures system audio output via WASAPI loopback at 48 kHz; (3) captured frames are preprocessed (silence filtering, resampling to 16 kHz, normalization); (4) two parallel feature extraction pipelines compute Mel Spectrograms and LFCC features; (5) the CNN model (via TFLite) processes Mel features while the GMM model processes LFCC features; (6) the Decision Engine aggregates both model outputs using weighted averaging and a 5-frame sliding window; and (7) the GUI displays the smoothed classification result with appropriate visual alerts.
	
	The architecture is event-driven and multi-threaded. The VoIP monitor runs in a daemon thread, the audio recorder runs in a separate daemon thread, and both inference engines support asynchronous background execution via dedicated worker threads. The GUI runs on the main thread and polls for results using a timer-based update mechanism. All inter-thread communication uses thread-safe queues and locks.

	\section{Proposed Methodology/Algorithms}
	
	\subsection{Mel Spectrogram Feature Extraction}
	\paragraph\ 
	Mel Spectrograms are computed from each 2.5-second audio window to serve as input to the CNN classifier. The extraction process follows these steps:
	
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item Compute the Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) with $N_{FFT} = 512$, hop length = 160 samples (10 ms), and window length = 400 samples (25 ms).
		\item Apply a 60-band Mel filterbank spanning 0 Hz to 8,000 Hz (Nyquist frequency for 16 kHz audio).
		\item Convert to log-power scale using:
	\end{enumerate}
	
	\begin{equation}
		S_{dB} = 10 \cdot \log_{10}\left(\frac{S_{mel}}{S_{ref}} + \epsilon\right)
	\end{equation}
	
	where $S_{ref}$ is the maximum value and $\epsilon = 10^{-10}$ prevents numerical underflow. The output is trimmed or padded to a fixed 79 time frames and normalized to $[0, 1]$ range, yielding a feature tensor of shape $(60 \times 79 \times 1)$.
	
	\subsection{LFCC Feature Extraction}
	\paragraph\ 
	Linear Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (LFCC) are extracted for the GMM classifier. Unlike Mel-scale features, LFCC uses linearly spaced filterbanks, which preserves details in the high-frequency range where AI-generated speech artifacts are most prominent. The extraction steps are:
	
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item Compute the STFT with $N_{FFT} = 2048$ and hop length = 512 samples.
		\item Apply an 80-filter linear filterbank uniformly spaced between 80 Hz and 7,500 Hz.
		\item Apply logarithmic compression: $\log(E + \epsilon)$ where $E$ is the filter energy.
		\item Apply the Type-II Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) to decorrelate the filter outputs:
	\end{enumerate}
	
	\begin{equation}
		c_k = \sqrt{\frac{2}{N}} \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} \log(E_n) \cos\left[\frac{\pi k (2n+1)}{2N}\right], \quad k = 0, 1, \ldots, 19
	\end{equation}
	
	The first 20 cepstral coefficients are retained, yielding a feature matrix of shape $(20 \times T)$ per audio frame, where $T$ is the number of time frames.
	
	\subsection{Lightweight CNN Architecture}
	\paragraph\ 
	The CNN is based on a MobileNet-inspired architecture using depthwise separable convolutions for parameter efficiency. The complete architecture is:
	
	\begin{table}[H]
		\begin{center}
			\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|}
				\hline
				\textbf{Layer} & \textbf{Type} & \textbf{Output Shape} & \textbf{Parameters} \\
				\hline
				Input & -- & $(60, 79, 1)$ & 0 \\
				\hline
				Block 1 & Conv2D + BN + ReLU6 & $(30, 40, 16)$ & 160 \\
				\hline
				Block 2 & DW-Sep Conv + Pool & $(15, 20, 32)$ & 688 \\
				\hline
				Block 3 & DW-Sep Conv + Pool & $(7, 10, 64)$ & 2,400 \\
				\hline
				Block 4 & DW-Sep Conv + Pool & $(3, 5, 128)$ & 9,024 \\
				\hline
				GAP & GlobalAvgPool2D & $(128)$ & 0 \\
				\hline
				Dense & Dense + ReLU6 + Dropout & $(64)$ & 8,256 \\
				\hline
				Output & Dense + Sigmoid & $(1)$ & 65 \\
				\hline
				\multicolumn{3}{|c|}{\textbf{Total}} & \textbf{$\sim$13,765} \\
				\hline
			\end{tabular}
			\caption{CNN model architecture}
		\end{center}
	\end{table}
	
	Each depthwise separable block consists of a depthwise convolution ($3 \times 3$ kernel, one filter per input channel) followed by a pointwise convolution ($1 \times 1$ kernel) that combines channels. This reduces parameters by approximately 8--9$\times$ compared to standard convolutions. ReLU6 activation ($\min(\max(x, 0), 6)$) is used throughout to facilitate accurate int8 quantization for TFLite deployment.
	
	The model is compiled with the Adam optimizer (learning rate = $10^{-3}$), binary cross-entropy loss with label smoothing of 0.1, and metrics including accuracy, precision, recall, and AUC.
	
	\subsection{Gaussian Mixture Model}
	\paragraph\ 
	Two independent GMMs are trained using the Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm via scikit-learn:
	\begin{itemize}
		\item $\text{GMM}_{human}$: Models the LFCC distribution of human speech.
		\item $\text{GMM}_{ai}$: Models the LFCC distribution of AI-generated speech.
	\end{itemize}
	
	During inference, the log-likelihood of the test LFCC features under each model is computed, and the difference is mapped to a probability using the sigmoid function:
	
	\begin{equation}
		P_{AI} = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-\alpha \cdot (\mathcal{L}_{AI} - \mathcal{L}_{human})}}
	\end{equation}
	
	where $\mathcal{L}_{AI}$ and $\mathcal{L}_{human}$ are the mean log-likelihoods per frame from each GMM, and $\alpha = 0.5$ is a scaling factor that controls sensitivity.
	
	\subsection{Ensemble Decision Engine}
	\paragraph\ 
	The hybrid decision engine combines CNN and GMM outputs using weighted averaging:
	
	\begin{equation}
		S_{ensemble} = w_{CNN} \cdot P_{CNN} + w_{GMM} \cdot P_{GMM}
	\end{equation}
	
	where $w_{CNN} = 0.60$ and $w_{GMM} = 0.40$. A sliding window buffer of size 5 stores recent ensemble scores, and the final classification is based on the buffer average:
	
	\begin{equation}
		\bar{S} = \frac{1}{|B|} \sum_{i \in B} S_{ensemble}^{(i)}
	\end{equation}
	
	Classification thresholds: $\bar{S} < 0.40 \rightarrow$ Human; $0.40 \leq \bar{S} < 0.70 \rightarrow$ Suspicious; $\bar{S} \geq 0.70 \rightarrow$ AI Generated.
	
	\subsection{Training Configuration}
	\paragraph\ 
	The CNN is trained with the following configuration:
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Batch size: 32
		\item Maximum epochs: 50
		\item Early stopping: Patience of 10 epochs on validation loss
		\item Learning rate reduction: Factor 0.5 with patience of 5 epochs (minimum $10^{-5}$)
		\item Label smoothing: 0.1 (targets become 0.05/0.95 instead of 0/1)
		\item Callbacks: ModelCheckpoint, EarlyStopping, ReduceLROnPlateau, TensorBoard, CSVLogger, custom EER callback
	\end{itemize}

	\section{Description of Implementation Strategies}
	\paragraph\
	The following key implementation strategies are employed:
	
	\subsection{Audio Capture via WASAPI Loopback}
	The \texttt{soundcard} Python library is used to access the default speaker's loopback microphone, enabling capture of all audio output from the system at the native sample rate (48 kHz). The captured stereo audio is converted to mono by averaging channels.
	
	\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python, caption=Audio capture using soundcard loopback]
import soundcard as sc
speaker = sc.default_speaker()
mic = sc.get_microphone(
    id=speaker.name, include_loopback=True)
with mic.recorder(samplerate=48000) as recorder:
    data = recorder.record(numframes=144000)  # 3 seconds
    mono = np.mean(data, axis=1)
	\end{lstlisting}
	
	\subsection{VoIP Detection via pycaw}
	The \texttt{pycaw} library provides access to Windows audio session information. Active audio sessions are enumerated and filtered for known VoIP process names with active audio state.
	
	\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python, caption=VoIP process detection]
from pycaw.pycaw import AudioUtilities
sessions = AudioUtilities.GetAllSessions()
for s in sessions:
    if s.State == 1:  # ACTIVE
        proc = s.Process
        if any(kw in proc.name().lower() 
               for kw in ['whatsapp','zoom','teams']):
            return True
	\end{lstlisting}
	
	\subsection{TFLite Inference}
	The trained CNN model is converted to TensorFlow Lite format with int8 quantization, reducing model size from several megabytes to under 400 KB. Inference is performed using the TFLite interpreter:
	
	\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python, caption=TFLite model inference]
import tensorflow as tf
interpreter = tf.lite.Interpreter(
    model_path='voice_classifier_v4.tflite')
interpreter.allocate_tensors()
input_details = interpreter.get_input_details()
interpreter.set_tensor(
    input_details[0]['index'], mel_features)
interpreter.invoke()
output = interpreter.get_tensor(
    output_details[0]['index'])
probability = float(output[0][0])
	\end{lstlisting}
	
	\subsection{RMS Normalization}
	All audio frames are normalized to a consistent RMS level of $-$20 dB to eliminate volume variations across different VoIP platforms and hardware configurations:
	
	\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python, caption=RMS normalization to -20 dB]
audio = audio - np.mean(audio)  # DC offset removal
rms = np.sqrt(np.mean(audio ** 2))
target_rms = 10 ** (-20 / 20)  # = 0.1
audio = audio * (target_rms / rms)
audio = np.clip(audio, -1.0, 1.0)
	\end{lstlisting}

	\section{Discussion}
	\paragraph\
	The system achieves strong performance on both controlled test data (98.6\% accuracy, 1.75\% EER) and real-world VoIP conditions (95.7\% combined accuracy). The most significant finding is the 100\% detection rate for AI-generated voices in real VoIP calls, indicating that synthetic speech artifacts survive VoIP codec compression and remain detectable by the system.
	
	The slightly lower accuracy on human speech (91.8\%) compared to AI speech detection is attributable to the domain gap between training data and real-world VoIP conditions. While the training data includes extensive Opus codec augmentation, the actual VoIP pipeline introduces additional variabilities such as acoustic echo cancellation, automatic gain control, and adaptive bitrate switching that are not fully captured in augmentation. The 3-tier classification system (Human/Suspicious/AI) mitigates this by providing a buffer zone for uncertain predictions rather than forcing binary decisions.
	
	The lightweight model architecture ($\sim$13,765 parameters) and TFLite deployment (under 400 KB) demonstrate that effective deepfake detection does not require the large transformer-based models commonly used in the literature. The depthwise separable convolution design reduces computational cost by 8--9$\times$ compared to standard convolutions while maintaining accuracy, enabling real-time performance on consumer hardware without GPU acceleration.
	
	\chapter{Conclusion}
	
	
	\section{Conclusion}
	\paragraph\
	This project successfully designed, implemented, and evaluated a real-time lightweight system for detecting AI-generated voices during active VoIP calls. The system operates entirely on the user's device, ensuring privacy and eliminating dependency on cloud infrastructure. By combining a lightweight CNN trained on Mel Spectrogram features with a GMM trained on LFCC features through an ensemble decision engine, the system achieves robust detection performance even under the challenging acoustic conditions introduced by VoIP codecs.
	
	The CNN model, built using depthwise separable convolutions inspired by the MobileNet architecture, achieves 98.6\% accuracy on held-out test data and 95.7\% accuracy on real-world WhatsApp VoIP recordings, with a 100\% detection rate for AI-generated voices in live call conditions. The entire model is deployable as a TFLite file under 400 KB, with inference completing in under 50 ms per 2.5-second audio window on consumer hardware without GPU acceleration. The 5-frame sliding window decision engine effectively smooths per-frame prediction noise, resulting in stable and reliable classification output suitable for real-time user-facing applications.
	
	The project demonstrates that effective deepfake voice detection does not require large-scale cloud-based models. A carefully designed lightweight architecture with appropriate training data augmentation (particularly VoIP codec simulation) and a hybrid ensemble approach can achieve practical detection performance in a fully on-device deployment. The system addresses a real and growing cybersecurity threat, providing an accessible tool for protecting users from AI-powered voice fraud.

		\item \textbf{Android Deployment}: The TFLite model can be deployed on Android smartphones using the TensorFlow Lite runtime, enabling detection during mobile VoIP calls. The Android AudioRecord API provides access to call audio on supported devices.
		\item \textbf{Continual Learning}: Implementing a mechanism to update the model with newly encountered AI voice samples would help the system adapt to evolving TTS technologies without requiring full retraining.
		\item \textbf{Cross-Platform Support}: Extending the audio capture module to support macOS (via CoreAudio) and Linux (via PulseAudio/PipeWire) would broaden the system's accessibility.
		\item \textbf{Multi-Language Support}: The current dataset is predominantly English. Training on multilingual datasets would improve detection accuracy for non-English VoIP calls.
		\item \textbf{Advanced Ensemble Methods}: Exploring learned fusion weights (e.g., attention-based aggregation of CNN and GMM scores) could further improve ensemble performance over the current fixed-weight approach.
	\end{enumerate}

	\textbf{Institute Vision} \\
	To evolve into a premier technological institution, moulding eminent professionals with creative minds, innovative ideas and sound practical skill, and to shape a future where technology works for the enrichment of mankind. \\ \\
	
	\textbf{Institute Mission} \\
	To impart state-of-the-art knowledge to individuals in various technological disciplines and to inculcate in them a high degree of social consciousness and human values, thereby enabling them to face the challenges of life with courage and conviction. \\ \\
	
	\textbf{Department Vision} \\
	To become a centre of excellence in Computer Science and Engineering, moulding professionals catering to the research and professional needs of national and international organizations. \\ \\
	
	\textbf{Department Mission} \\
	To inspire and nurture students, with up-to-date knowledge in Computer Science and Engineering, ethics, team spirit, leadership abilities, innovation and creativity to come out with solutions meeting societal needs. \\ \\
	
	\textbf{Programme Outcomes (PO)} \\
	Engineering Graduates will be able to: \\ \\
	\textbf{PO1: Engineering Knowledge}: Apply knowledge of mathematics, natural science, computing, engineering fundamentals and an engineering specialization as specified in WK1 to WK4 respectively to develop to the solution of complex engineering problems.  \\ \\
	\textbf{PO2: Problem Analysis}: Identify, formulate, review research literature and analyze complex engineering problems reaching substantiated conclusions with consideration for sustainable development. \\ \\
	\textbf{PO3: Design/Development of Solutions}: Design creative solutions for complex engineering problems and design/develop systems/components/processes to meet identified needs with consideration for the public health and safety, whole-life cost, net zero carbon, culture, society and environment as required. \\ \\
	\textbf{PO4: Conduct Investigations of Complex Problems}: Conduct investigations of complex engineering problems using research-based knowledge including design of experiments, modelling, analysis and interpretation of data to provide valid conclusions. \\ \\
	\textbf{PO5: Engineering Tool Usage}: Create, select and apply appropriate techniques, resources and modern engineering and IT tools, including prediction and modelling recognizing their limitations to solve complex engineering problems. \\ \\
	\textbf{PO6: The Engineer and The World}: Analyze and evaluate societal and environmental aspects while solving complex engineering problems for its impact on sustainability with reference to economy, health, safety, legal framework, culture and environment. \\ \\
	\textbf{PO7: Ethics}: Apply ethical principles and commit to professional ethics, human values, diversity and inclusion; adhere to national and international laws.  \\ \\
	\textbf{PO8: Individual and Collaborative Team work}: Function effectively as an individual, and as a member or leader in diverse/multi-disciplinary teams.  \\ \\
	\textbf{PO9: Communication}:Communicate effectively and inclusively within the engineering community and society at large, such as being able to comprehend and write effective reports and design documentation, make effective presentations considering cultural, language, and learning differences. \\ \\
	\textbf{PO10: Project Management and Finance}: Apply knowledge and understanding of engineering management principles and economic decision-making and apply these to one’s own work, as a member and leader in a team, and to manage projects and in multidisciplinary environments. \\ \\
	\textbf{PO11: Life-Long Learning}: Recognize the need for, and have the preparation and ability for i) independent and life-long learning ii) adaptability to new and emerging technologies and iii) critical thinking in the broadest context of technological change.  \\ \\
